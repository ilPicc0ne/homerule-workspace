HomeRule

A model has a training cutoff. A law has an effective date.

Housing law compiled into checkable, dated rules. Every address gets a quoted answer, or one honest question. The same engine serves renters on the web and any AI assistant through MCP.

Working name · concept from the deep-work review, Sat 03.10. 20:30 Freeze Sun 12:00 · Submit Sun 15:00 –

Problem and solution

Problem

A renter with a notice or a charge has days to decide whether to push back. The answer depends on three layers of law, the building's age and size, and dates that move every few months. Tenants have a lawyer in about 3–4% of eviction cases. The chatbot they reach for:

  • cannot know laws passed after its training,
  • confuses pending, struck and not-yet-effective law,
  • does not know that a "Los Angeles" mailing address can lie outside the city.

Where knowing the rules moves money: a rent increase (pay or contest the cap), a deposit (demand it back; MA treble, CA up to ×2), an application fee (NJ cap $50 from 5/1/2026), an eviction notice (does just cause apply?).

Why it is different

All three simulated crowds build the same six products: RAG chatbot, address dashboard, change tracker, date-slider map, bilingual card, landlord view. They differ in the UI, not in the 75 scripted points, and none of them measures anything.

Discipline on the traps

Legal city, verbatim quotes, local over state, statuses, misses costing double, the 19 "no rule" findings. The 75 script points come by construction.

A measured contrast

The crowd's architecture (an LLM decides coverage) against ours, on the judges' own score.py and ~25 dated trap questions. It answers "why not just ask ChatGPT?" with a number.
